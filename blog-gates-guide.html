Custom Gates: Wood, Wrought Iron, or Aluminum — What's Right for Your Property?

A gate is the entry point and the first impression. Here's how to choose one that holds up and looks the part for years.

Gates December 3, 2025 7 min read

Most people think about the fence first and treat the gate as an afterthought. That's backwards. The gate is the thing people actually interact with — they touch it, open it, walk through it. It's the functional entry point of the whole fenced area, and it's the first thing a visitor sees when approaching your property.

A gate that sags, drags, or doesn't latch right is frustrating every single day. A gate built correctly from the right material is something you stop noticing entirely because it just works. Here's how to make the right call on material and design before anything gets installed.

The Most Common Gate Failure — and How to Avoid It

The number one cause of gate problems isn't material — it's post installation. Gates are heavy and experience constant leverage stress every time they swing open. If the posts aren't set deep enough with adequate concrete, they lean over time. Once a post leans, the gate drags, the latch misaligns, and the whole thing becomes a problem.

In Indiana, gate posts need to go below the 36-inch frost line — the same standard as deck footings. This isn't optional. A post set at 24 inches will heave after two or three winters, and no amount of latch adjustment will fix a structural problem. Every gate we install gets proper post depth as a baseline requirement.

Wood Gates — Warmth, Privacy, and Natural Character

Wood gates are the natural complement to a wood privacy fence. They match the material, carry the same warmth and texture, and can be built in virtually any style — board-on-board, horizontal slat, framed panel, arched top, flat top, or custom cut.

  • Appearance: Warm, natural, customizable in profile and finish
  • Privacy: Full privacy possible with solid panel construction
  • Lifespan: 15–20 years with proper maintenance; cedar performs longer than pressure-treated
  • Maintenance: Seal or stain every 2–3 years to prevent warping and rot. Hinges and latches should be checked annually and lubricated
  • Limitations: Wide single-panel gates (anything over 4 feet) are prone to racking and sagging over time. These should be built with a diagonal brace in the frame — not just horizontal rails
  • Best for: Residential privacy gates, yard entry, matching an existing wood fence

The width of the gate matters a lot for wood. For openings wider than 5 feet, we typically recommend double gates (two panels meeting in the center) rather than a single heavy panel. A wide single wood gate without proper steel reinforcement will sag — it's just a matter of when.

Wrought Iron & Ornamental Steel — Security and Presence

Wrought iron gates are the premium statement option. They're heavy, they're strong, and they project a level of permanence that no other gate material matches. Properly coated, an ornamental steel gate will outlast the fence it's attached to.

  • Appearance: Bold, architectural, traditional or contemporary depending on design. Visible property line definition without blocking the view
  • Durability: 30–50 years with proper rust protection
  • Maintenance: Inspect annually for rust at welded joints and scratched coating. Touch up with rust-inhibiting paint or spray where bare metal is exposed. Powder-coated finishes last significantly longer than paint
  • Security: Hardest to defeat of the three options — the weight and rigidity of steel make forced entry considerably more difficult than wood or aluminum
  • Weight: Heavy — requires robust posts and commercial-grade hinges to swing smoothly over time
  • Best for: Driveway entrances, front property gates, pool enclosures, pairing with ornamental fencing

Iron and steel gates are often paired with a different fence material — wood privacy on the sides and back, ornamental gate and fence across the front. It's a practical and attractive combination that gives you privacy where you need it and curb appeal at the street.

Aluminum — Low Maintenance, Rust-Free, Commercial-Ready

Aluminum gates look similar to ornamental steel at a distance but weigh significantly less and are entirely rust-proof. They're a strong choice for homeowners who want the open, architectural look of iron without the maintenance concerns — or for commercial properties that need a durable gate with minimal upkeep.

  • Appearance: Clean, geometric, available in a range of styles and powder-coat colors
  • Weight: Significantly lighter than steel — easier on posts and hinges, but also less substantial feeling
  • Rust resistance: Aluminum doesn't rust. It can oxidize over decades but won't fail the way steel does without maintenance
  • Maintenance: Almost none — rinse occasionally, check hardware annually
  • Limitations: Less rigid than steel; a hard impact can bend aluminum panels where steel would simply scratch. Not the right choice for high-security applications
  • Best for: Pool enclosures, commercial properties, decorative residential gates, coastal or high-moisture environments

Automated vs. Manual — What's Worth It

Gate automation is a real upgrade in convenience for driveways and frequently used vehicle entrances. It's not for every situation, but if you're installing a driveway gate and will be using it daily, the cost is usually worth it.

A few honest notes on automation:

  • Automation adds complexity — motors, control boards, safety sensors, and power supply all need to be reliable and properly installed
  • Most quality residential gate operators (like LiftMaster or Viking Access) are robust and low-maintenance with a 5–10 year lifespan
  • The gate itself needs to be built for automation — correct weight, proper balance, and posts rated for the motor load
  • If you lose power, you need a manual release. That should be part of the installation from day one

For pedestrian gates and yard access, manual operation is almost always the better choice. Automation adds cost and a potential failure point on a gate you'll rarely drive through.

Matching Your Gate to Your Fence

The strongest-looking gate installations are cohesive — the gate reads as part of the fence system, not an afterthought bolted on later. A few guidelines:

  • Wood fence → wood gate: Same material, same board profile, same finish. The simplest and cleanest solution
  • Chain link fence → chain link gate: Standard galvanized or color-coated to match; aluminum-framed chain link is a common upgrade
  • Mixed approach: Wood privacy fence with an ornamental iron gate at the driveway or entrance — this works well and is a common choice we see on residential properties in the area
  • Standalone gates: If you need a single gate in a hedge or masonry wall, ornamental steel or aluminum are the most practical choices — they don't require a fence run to tie into
